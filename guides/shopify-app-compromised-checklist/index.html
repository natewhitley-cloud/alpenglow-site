What to check on your store after a Shopify app is reported compromised

Updated October 12, 2026

Short answer: first find out whether the app is, or ever was, on your store. If it is, test your checkout in a private browser window on a desktop and on a phone, and confirm the address bar stays on your own store's domain. Then uninstall the app if you can't trust it, review what your other apps can access, search your theme for code the app left behind, change the passwords that matter, and tell the app's developer and Shopify Support what you found.

One step on this list, searching your theme for leftover app code, can be automated. GhostCode does that part. It does not test your checkout and it can't see code that an installed app loads while a page is running, so it is not a substitute for the steps below.

What happened in October 2026

According to a developer's write-up published 2026-10-10, a shopper clicked Checkout on a Shopify store and landed on a page that looked like Shopify's checkout but sat on a different web address. The author traced it to a script file named hulkcode.js, which he says is delivered from Shopify's CDN and appears to be authored by the app developer HulkApps. He reports that when a page loaded, this script requested data from the developer's server, that the response included custom JavaScript, and that this JavaScript ran in the shopper's browser and redirected the checkout click to a fake checkout page. The fake page collected what the shopper typed and then passed them on to the real checkout.

Three details from the write-up matter for the checklist below:

  • The code was not in the store's theme. As described, it arrived from the app developer's server each time a page loaded.
  • It acted once per visitor. The write-up says the script recorded in the browser's session storage that a shopper had already been redirected, and did not try again.
  • The cause was not established. The author says it was unclear how the code got there and lists several possibilities, from a compromised merchant account at the developer to a compromise of the developer itself. He knew of two affected stores at the time of writing.

The write-up also reports that Shopify escalated the issue the same day, that the redirect stopped on the store where it was first seen, and that the store confirmed it had removed the app. We have not verified the write-up's findings ourselves, and we don't know the developer's account of what happened.

The checklist

1. Find out whether the app is on your store

In your Shopify admin, go to Settings → Apps. The Installed tab lists what is on your store now and the Uninstalled tab lists what has been removed. Click an app's name to see its history of installs and uninstalls. Check for other apps from the same developer as well. If the app was never on your store, the report doesn't apply to you, though steps 2 and 4 are worth doing a few times a year anyway.

2. Test your checkout the way a new shopper would

  1. Open a new private or incognito window. This matters: by the write-up's account the redirect happened once per visitor, so a browser that has already been through your checkout may not show it.
  2. Go to your store, add any product to the cart and click Checkout.
  3. Read the address bar before you type anything. It should show your store's own domain: the one customers normally see, or your myshopify.com address. You can see every domain connected to your store under Settings → Domains.
  4. Repeat on a phone, again in a fresh private window. Then repeat with each checkout button you offer, including the cart drawer and any Buy now button on the product page.

If you offer Shop Pay, a step that asks the shopper to sign in or enter a code from Shop is part of how Shop Pay works. That step is on Shop's own site (shop.app) and returns the shopper to your checkout.

Stop if the address is anything else, especially a lookalike with a misspelled word or an unfamiliar ending. Don't enter real card details. Take a screenshot showing the full address and go to step 3.

3. Uninstall the app if you can't trust it right now

Go to Settings → Apps, open the menu next to the app and choose Uninstall. Uninstalling ends the app's access to your store, and Shopify removes the app's blocks, embeds and script tags from your storefront. Before you do it, export any data you need from the app, because some app data can't be recovered afterwards. If the app charges you outside Shopify, cancel that with the developer too.

Then run the checkout test from step 2 again, in a new private window.

4. Review what your other apps can access

Still in Settings → Apps, click each installed app. The Activity and permissions section shows which areas of your store the app can view or edit and when it was last active in each. The Privacy section shows which personal data it can access. Uninstall apps you no longer use. Every installed app that loads code on your storefront is something you are trusting on every page view, so a shorter list is a smaller risk.

5. Look for code the app left in your theme

Uninstalling does not remove code that an app wrote into your theme files, or code you pasted in when you set it up. Go to Online Store, open the menu (…) on your theme and choose Edit code, then use the search button in the left sidebar to search every file for the app's name and its web address. Any line that still loads a script from the developer's servers keeps running in your shoppers' browsers after the app is gone.

If you want to see what a file looked like earlier, the code editor's Timeline view shows previous versions of one file at a time. Its history is limited and it doesn't cover files in the assets folder.

More detail: what uninstalling an app does and doesn't remove, and how to remove leftover app code safely.

6. Check Customer events

Go to Settings → Customer events → Custom pixels. Custom pixels are pieces of code that you or someone working on your store added by hand. Disconnect or delete any you don't recognize or no longer need.

7. Change the passwords that matter

  • Your account with the app's developer, if you have a separate login there. Change the password and turn on two-step sign-in if they offer it. The write-up's author gives the same advice.
  • Keys you gave the app for other services, such as an email platform or an ads account. Create new keys in those services and delete the old ones.
  • Your Shopify login, only if there are signs it was accessed: sign-ins you don't recognize, new staff accounts, or settings that changed. In that case Shopify's guidance is to change the password on your email account and your Shopify account, set up two-step authentication again, review staff permissions and your store's activity log, check your Checkout, Domains and Notifications settings, and look over your Shopify financial accounts for activity you don't recognize.

You don't need to do anything about the app's own access to Shopify. Uninstalling the app ended it.

8. Tell the right people

  • The app's developer. Use the support contact on the app's App Store listing. Say what you saw and when.
  • Shopify Support. Start a chat from help.shopify.com. Give the app's name, your screenshots and the address shoppers were sent to. Shopify normally sends questions about a third-party app to its developer, but a redirected checkout is still worth reporting: in the incident above the author reports that Shopify acted the same day. If you think your Shopify account itself was accessed, Shopify says to contact Support immediately.
  • Your payment provider and an adviser, if shoppers may have typed card details into a fake page. Depending on where you and your customers are, you may have a duty to notify customers or a regulator. This guide can't tell you whether you do. Ask someone qualified before you decide.

Common questions

How do I test whether my Shopify checkout is being redirected?

Open a new private or incognito window, add a product to the cart, click Checkout, and read the address bar before you type anything. The address should be on your store's own domain. Do it once on a desktop browser and once on a phone, each time in a fresh private window.

Does uninstalling a compromised Shopify app fix the problem?

It ends the app's access to your store, and Shopify removes the app's blocks, embeds and script tags. It does not remove code the app wrote into your theme files, and it does not undo anything that already happened. After uninstalling, repeat the checkout test and search your theme for leftover code.

Would a theme code scan detect a compromised app?

Not if the harmful code is delivered by the installed app while the page loads, which is what a developer's write-up described in the October 2026 checkout redirect incident. That code is never stored in your theme files, so a theme scan cannot see it. A theme scan finds code that sits in your theme. The checkout test is the check that catches a redirect.

Do I need to change my Shopify password after an app is compromised?

A compromised app does not mean your Shopify login was exposed. Change the password for any account you hold with the app's developer first. Change your Shopify password and set up two-step authentication again if you see sign-ins, staff accounts or settings changes you don't recognize.

Who do I contact if I think an app on my store is compromised?

Contact the app's developer and Shopify Support, and tell both exactly what you saw, with screenshots and the web address shoppers were sent to. If you think your Shopify account itself was accessed, Shopify's guidance is to contact Shopify Support immediately.

For step 5 only: install GhostCode from the Shopify App Store to scan your theme for leftover app code. The first scan is free and it never edits your theme. Do the checkout test yourself first.
